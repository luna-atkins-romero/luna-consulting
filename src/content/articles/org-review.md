---

Every organisation aims to operate smoothly and deliver maximum impact. But over time — especially as teams grow, strategies evolve, or workloads increase — the way you're organised might stop serving those goals as well as it should. An organisational review is a chance to take a clear, objective look at how your structure, roles and workflows are working in practice so you can make thoughtful decisions about what comes next.

Here are four unmistakable signals that now might be the right time to commission a review:

# 1. Your team structure no longer reflects reality

Growth is a good problem to have — but unplanned growth can create confusion. When new roles and responsibilities have been added over time without regular check-ins on reporting lines or role clarity, you can end up with overlapping duties, unclear leadership and inefficiencies that slow everything down. A review helps you step back and design a structure that actually supports your mission and future ambitions.

# 2. Day-to-day work feels harder than it should

If collaboration feels clunky, communications break down, or processes that used to work now feel frustratingly slow, that's a sign your organisation's design isn't keeping up with reality. Effective organisational design isn't just about boxes on an org chart; it's about how people work together, communicate and make decisions. When those patterns aren't aligned with strategy, performance suffers.

# 3. Your team is constantly falling behind

When workloads have grown faster than your capacity to deliver, stress and delays become the norm. If you're repeatedly missing deadlines or struggling to meet expectations — even though priorities are clear — it's time to look honestly at whether you have the right capacity and capability in the right places. An organisational review helps you map where gaps exist and how best to address them.

# 4. You're relying heavily on temporary or external help

Contractors, agency staff and consultants can be incredibly useful — but if they're essentially filling gaps that should belong to your core team, that's a cost and sustainability issue.  Leaning too much on short-term resources can mask deeper structural problems. A review gives you space to diagnose the root causes and develop a cost-effective, long-term plan.

# Bottom line

An organisational review isn't about change for change's sake.  It's about being deliberate — creating clarity, building capacity and ensuring your organisation is set up to deliver its strategy with confidence. When you recognise these patterns in your organisation, it's not a sign of failure — it's a sign you're ready to invest in stronger performance and sustainable impact.
